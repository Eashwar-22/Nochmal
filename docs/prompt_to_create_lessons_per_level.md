
Create a complete curriculum blueprint for CEFR [A1 / A2 / B1] German for Nochmal.

Do not integrate anything into the app and do not write full lesson passages yet.

First read the project’s curriculum, grammar, and lesson-format rules. Use the CEFR communicative descriptors and an appropriate Goethe vocabulary reference as coverage inputs, but do not treat either as a fixed lesson sequence.

Create a staged, learner-first plan that aims for broad practical coverage of this level: communication, vocabulary, grammar in context, listening, reading, guided speaking, guided writing, recycling, and checkpoints.

Organize the level into coherent modules. Sequence by communicative need, not by textbook grammar order. Introduce at most one substantial new structure per lesson. Recycle important vocabulary and forms several times across different situations, with increasing spacing. Include consolidation lessons that introduce little or nothing new.

Plan an explicit progression in passage length, sentence count, and connectedness. Do not make texts longer merely for the sake of length. Increase complexity through linked ideas, connectors, reference words, varied sentence openings, and longer uninterrupted listening or reading.

Use these planning ranges rather than rigid quotas:

- early A1: 3–4 short sentences, roughly 25–45 words;
- late A1: 4–6 linked sentences or a short dialogue, roughly 50–80 words;
- A2: 5–8 sentences or a short message, story, or dialogue, roughly 80–130 words;
- B1: 1–3 connected paragraphs, roughly 130–220 words.

Plan an intentional progression and distribution of sentence functions, not only declarative statements. Across the level, introduce and recycle:

- statements;
- W-questions;
- yes/no questions;
- short answers;
- imperatives;
- requests and polite questions;
- common everyday formulae;
- increasingly connected clauses.

Introduce each sentence function in a real communicative situation. Learners must first understand it in input and later use it in a speaking or writing task. Do not add sentence types as isolated grammar demonstrations.

For every proposed lesson, provide only:

- lesson number and short title;
- module;
- real-life situation;
- one learner “can do” outcome;
- primary language structure;
- sentence function(s) practised;
- target passage format, sentence range, and approximate word range;
- how it becomes more connected than earlier lessons, where applicable;
- 5–8 new vocabulary items or chunks;
- deliberately recycled items, with earlier lesson references;
- one short listening, reading, speaking, or writing performance/checkpoint activity.

Audit the finished blueprint for:

- CEFR can-do coverage;
- vocabulary coverage;
- grammar coverage in meaningful contexts;
- retrieval and spaced repetition;
- listening, reading, speaking, and writing coverage;
- balanced sentence-function coverage;
- a gradual progression from isolated sentences to connected texts;
- appropriate passage length and listening/reading stamina for the level;
- no sudden jump in sentence complexity or text length;
- no over-reliance on descriptive declarative sentences;
- a realistic final “can do” checkpoint for the level.

Keep the blueprint unpublished and separate from the app. Flag gaps, uncertainties, and decisions for my review instead of inventing hidden assumptions.
