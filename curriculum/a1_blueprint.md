# Nochmal CEFR A1 curriculum blueprint

> **Status: DRAFT — UNPUBLISHED PLANNING ARTIFACT.** This file is not imported by the app, does not define production lesson IDs, and does not authorize publication. No passage text, sentence annotations, breakdown cards, or audio is included. Every future lesson remains subject to the full sentence audit and publication gate in `docs/grammar-breakdown-rulebook.md` and `docs/lesson-format.md`.

## Scope and evidence

This blueprint proposes 48 lessons in eight modules. The sequence is learner-first: immediate interaction and communication repair come before systematic topic or grammar coverage. Every lesson has one main communicative outcome and no more than one substantial new structure. Lesson 6 in each module is a consolidation or checkpoint lesson with no new substantial grammar and only five low-load chunks; those chunks support the performance but are not checkpoint criteria.

Coverage inputs:

- The [CEFR global A1 descriptor](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-%201-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) defines the outer outcome: concrete everyday needs, personal details, and simple supported interaction.
- The Council of Europe [CEFR Companion Volume descriptor scales](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-%20with-new-descriptors-2018/1680787989) inform the listening, reading, spoken interaction, spoken production, written interaction, and written production outcomes. They are coverage criteria, not a German grammar sequence.
- The Goethe-Institut [Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 wordlist](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/de/A1_SD1_Wortliste_02.pdf) supplies the adult A1 lexical boundary and topic inventory. It contains about 650 entries, expects all broadly receptively and about half actively, and explicitly says it is a reference rather than material for sequencing and consolidation.
- The Goethe-Institut [official A1 practice materials](https://www.goethe.de/en/m/spr/prf/ueb/pa1.html) inform realistic task shapes across listening, reading, writing, and speaking. The blueprint is a practical curriculum, not an exam-preparation sequence.

All lesson passages remain audio-first in the Nochmal learning loop. The performance activity below is the additional retrieval task after comprehension and breakdown work.

## Module 1 — First contact and communication repair

### Lesson 1 — Hello, I’m …

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Meeting a neighbour or course participant for the first time.
- **Can do:** I can greet someone, say my name, and take leave.
- **Primary language structure:** First-person `ich` with `sein` and `heißen` in short verb-second statements.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Everyday formulae; statements; short reciprocal responses.
- **Target passage:** Micro-dialogue; 3 short sentences/turns; about 25–30 words.
- **Connectedness:** Baseline: formulaic turns and one short self-introduction; no connected text expected yet.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Hallo`; `Guten Tag`; `ich heiße …`; `ich bin …`; `Tschüs`; `Auf Wiedersehen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** None; entry lesson.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Select the speaker’s name and whether the exchange is opening or closing after one slow, uninterrupted listen.

### Lesson 2 — Where Are You From?

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Exchanging origin and place of residence at a course welcome table.
- **Can do:** I can ask and answer where someone comes from and lives.
- **Primary language structure:** W-question order with `wie`, `wo`, and `woher`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** W-questions; short answers; statements; greeting formulae.
- **Target passage:** Short dialogue; 3 sentences/turns; about 28–34 words.
- **Connectedness:** A question now directly prompts an answer, creating the first adjacency pair.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `woher`; `kommen`; `aus`; `wo`; `wohnen`; `die Stadt`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `Hallo`, `ich heiße …`, `ich bin …`, `Tschüs` (L1).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Use two prompt cards to ask for and give a name, origin, and current city.

### Lesson 3 — Please Say It Again

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Repairing a misunderstanding in a first German conversation.
- **Can do:** I can signal that I do not understand and ask for slow repetition.
- **Primary language structure:** Fixed polite repair chunks with `bitte`; no new clause pattern.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Simple requests; polite questions; everyday formulae; short answers.
- **Target passage:** Repair dialogue; 3–4 short sentences/turns; about 28–36 words.
- **Connectedness:** One turn now refers back to the previous turn and triggers repetition.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Wie bitte?`; `noch einmal, bitte`; `langsam, bitte`; `Ich verstehe nicht.`; `Was bedeutet …?`; `Danke`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `wie` (L2); `Guten Tag`, `ich heiße …` (L1); `woher kommen` (L2).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Hear three brief misunderstandings and choose the appropriate repair chunk for each.

### Lesson 4 — Is That Correct?

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Confirming a name and contact detail at reception.
- **Can do:** I can check a detail and answer yes or no.
- **Primary language structure:** Yes/no question order with `sein`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Yes/no questions; short answers; polite request; confirmation formulae.
- **Target passage:** Reception exchange; 3–4 sentences/turns; about 30–38 words.
- **Connectedness:** A detail is stated, checked, and confirmed across three dependent turns.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Telefonnummer`; `die E-Mail`; `der Buchstabe`; `buchstabieren`; `richtig`; `falsch`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `ich heiße …` (L1); `Wie bitte?`, `noch einmal, bitte`, `Danke` (L3); `wo` (L2).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Complete four fields on a simple contact card after checking the spelling shown in a short prompt.

### Lesson 5 — This Is My Friend

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Introducing a companion at a small social gathering.
- **Can do:** I can introduce another person and ask who someone is.
- **Primary language structure:** Third-person pronouns `er` and `sie` with `sein` and `heißen`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; W-question; yes/no question; short answers; introduction formulae.
- **Target passage:** Short social dialogue; 3–4 sentences/turns; about 32–40 words.
- **Connectedness:** `er` and `sie` refer back to named people instead of repeating names.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `der Freund`; `die Freundin`; `der Mann`; `die Frau`; `wer`; `Das ist …`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `Hallo`, `ich bin …` (L1); `woher kommen`, `wohnen` (L2); `richtig` (L4).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Match three very short introductions to three labelled people, using names, cities, and pronouns.

### Lesson 6 — Checkpoint: First Conversation

- **Module:** 1 — First contact and communication repair
- **Real-life situation:** Meeting someone new when one detail is not understood.
- **Can do:** I can complete a supported first conversation and repair one misunderstanding.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of first/third person, W-questions, and yes/no questions; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Greetings; statements; W-questions; yes/no questions; short answers; repair requests; leave-taking.
- **Target passage:** Short dialogue; 4 sentences/turns; about 34–42 words.
- **Connectedness:** The exchange sustains one topic across greeting, information, repair, confirmation, and closing.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Freut mich.`; `Wie geht’s?`; `Gut, danke.`; `auch`; `leider`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `ich heiße …`, `Auf Wiedersehen` (L1); `woher kommen`, `wohnen` (L2); `noch einmal, bitte`, `Ich verstehe nicht.` (L3); `richtig` (L4); `Das ist …` (L5).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Complete a 60–90 second role-play with a name, city, introduced companion, one confirmation, and one repair request.

## Module 2 — Personal world, time, and routine

### Lesson 7 — My Family

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Showing a family photo to a classmate.
- **Can do:** I can name close family members and say who they are.
- **Primary language structure:** Possessive determiners `mein/meine` and `dein/deine` in nominative noun phrases.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; W-questions; short answers.
- **Target passage:** Photo conversation; 3–4 sentences/turns; about 30–40 words.
- **Connectedness:** Several statements stay on one person network, with pronouns linking back to the photo.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Familie`; `die Mutter`; `der Vater`; `der Bruder`; `die Schwester`; `das Kind`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `wer`, `Das ist …`, `er`, `sie` (L5); `heißen`, `wohnen` (L1–L2).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Identify the correct family photo from a four-sentence caption.

### Lesson 8 — Personal Details

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Giving basic details on a course registration form.
- **Can do:** I can give my age, address, and date of birth in a simple form.
- **Primary language structure:** Cardinal numbers and date chunks used as personal information.
- **Sentence functions practised:** W-questions; short answers; written labels and formulae.
- **Target passage:** Registration interview plus form labels; 3–4 sentences/turns; about 32–42 words.
- **Connectedness:** Spoken answers map to related written fields, linking dialogue and form.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `das Jahr`; `alt`; `die Adresse`; `das Geburtsdatum`; `der Geburtstag`; `die Postleitzahl`; `geboren`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `die Telefonnummer`, `die E-Mail`, `buchstabieren` (L4); `Wie geht’s?` (L6); `die Familie` (L7).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Fill in six personal-data fields from a learner profile, including a date and postcode.

### Lesson 9 — My Morning

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Describing a normal morning before work or class.
- **Can do:** I can say when I get up, eat, and start work or study.
- **Primary language structure:** Separable verbs in present-tense main clauses.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; simple sequence markers.
- **Target passage:** Short routine; 3–4 sentences; about 34–44 words.
- **Connectedness:** Events form a chronological mini-sequence with `dann` rather than isolated facts.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `aufstehen`; `frühstücken`; `anfangen`; `die Arbeit`; `die Schule`; `früh`; `dann`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `wohnen` (L2); `die Familie` (L7); numbers (L8); `ich bin …` (L1).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Put four morning actions in order after one complete slow recording.

### Lesson 10 — What Time?

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Checking the time of a class and an appointment.
- **Can do:** I can ask for and understand a time.
- **Primary language structure:** Clock-time phrases with `um` and sentence-initial time expressions with verb-second order.
- **Sentence functions practised:** W-questions; yes/no questions; short answers; statements.
- **Target passage:** Schedule exchange; 3–4 sentences/turns; about 34–44 words.
- **Connectedness:** Two events are contrasted by time, and a time-first sentence varies the opening.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Uhr`; `halb`; `Viertel vor`; `Viertel nach`; `der Termin`; `pünktlich`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** numbers and dates (L8); `anfangen`, `die Schule`, `die Arbeit` (L9); confirmation questions (L4).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Mark the correct start time and appointment time on a two-line schedule.

### Lesson 11 — Are You Free?

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Finding a shared time for coffee.
- **Can do:** I can ask about availability and agree on a day and time.
- **Primary language structure:** Modal `können` plus a sentence-final infinitive.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Yes/no questions; W-question; short answers; simple suggestion; acceptance and refusal.
- **Target passage:** Arrangement dialogue; 4 sentences/turns; about 36–45 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each response narrows the options until the speakers agree on one plan.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `heute`; `morgen`; `der Montag`; `der Dienstag`; `frei`; `Zeit haben`; `sich treffen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `der Termin`, clock times (L10); confirmation questions (L4); `leider` (L6); `die Arbeit` (L9).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Compare two simple calendars and agree on one meeting time.

### Lesson 12 — Checkpoint: A Week at a Glance

- **Module:** 2 — Personal world, time, and routine
- **Real-life situation:** Telling a course partner about a normal week and arranging one meeting.
- **Can do:** I can give a short supported account of my week and make one arrangement.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of present tense, separable verbs, time-first order, and `können`; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; W-questions; yes/no questions; short answers; suggestion and response.
- **Target passage:** Linked routine plus two-turn exchange; 4 sentences/turns; about 40–45 words.
- **Connectedness:** `am Morgen`, `am Abend`, and `aber` connect recurring events and a scheduling contrast.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `am Morgen`; `am Abend`; `jeden Tag`; `oft`; `am Wochenende`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** family words (L7); `aufstehen`, `anfangen`, `dann` (L9); time phrases (L10); days, `frei`, `sich treffen` (L11).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing checkpoint:** Write four linked sentences about a weekly routine and one question proposing a meeting time.

## Module 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions

### Lesson 13 — At the Bakery

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Buying breakfast at a bakery counter.
- **Can do:** I can politely ask for a simple food item.
- **Primary language structure:** `ich möchte` plus an accusative indefinite noun phrase.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Polite requests; service formulae; short answers; statements.
- **Target passage:** Counter dialogue; 4 sentences/turns; about 40–48 words.
- **Connectedness:** The request, clarification, confirmation, and thanks form a complete transaction.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `möchten`; `das Brot`; `das Brötchen`; `der Käse`; `die Butter`; `nehmen`; `bitte`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** greetings and `Danke` (L1, L3); numbers (L8); yes/no confirmation (L4); `frühstücken` (L9).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Choose the customer’s items and quantity from a small picture menu.

### Lesson 14 — How Much Is It?

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Asking about price and quantity at a market stall.
- **Can do:** I can ask how much something costs and understand a simple price or quantity.
- **Primary language structure:** W-questions with `was` and `wie viel` for price and quantity.
- **Sentence functions practised:** W-questions; short answers; polite requests; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Market dialogue; 4 sentences/turns; about 40–50 words.
- **Connectedness:** A second question follows the first answer to complete a purchase decision.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `kosten`; `der Euro`; `das Kilo`; `das Stück`; `die Flasche`; `billig`; `teuer`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `ich möchte`, `nehmen`, `bitte` (L13); numbers (L8); `richtig` (L4).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Buy two items from a price card, asking one price and one quantity question.

### Lesson 15 — Breakfast Choices

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Talking about breakfast preferences at home or in a café.
- **Can do:** I can say what I eat and drink and what I prefer.
- **Primary language structure:** `gern` and `lieber` with present-tense activities.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; yes/no questions; short answers; preference formulae.
- **Target passage:** Short conversation; 4 sentences/turns; about 42–50 words.
- **Connectedness:** `aber` contrasts two people’s preferences; `er/sie` maintain reference.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `essen`; `trinken`; `der Kaffee`; `der Tee`; `die Milch`; `gern`; `lieber`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** breakfast items (L13); family members (L7); quantities (L14); `aber` (L12).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Match four short preference statements to two breakfast trays and two people.

### Lesson 16 — The Shopping List

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Checking what is and is not at home before grocery shopping.
- **Can do:** I can say what I need and what I do not have.
- **Primary language structure:** Noun negation with `kein/keine`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; yes/no questions; short answers; simple need statements.
- **Target passage:** Home exchange plus list; 4 sentences/turns; about 42–52 words.
- **Connectedness:** Pronouns refer back to list items, and `und/aber` join available and missing goods.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `brauchen`; `haben`; `die Tomate`; `der Apfel`; `das Gemüse`; `das Wasser`; `kein/keine`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `das Brot`, `die Butter`, `die Milch` (L13, L15); numbers and quantities (L8, L14); `essen`, `trinken` (L15).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Complete a six-item shopping list from four sentences about what is missing at home.

### Lesson 17 — At the Café

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Ordering, responding to a service question, and asking to pay.
- **Can do:** I can order a drink or simple meal and ask for the bill.
- **Primary language structure:** Recycled `können` in polite service questions: `Kann ich …?`.

- **Target passage:** Café dialogue; 4–5 sentences/turns; about 45–55 words.

- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Speisekarte`; `bestellen`; `das Essen`; `die Rechnung`; `zahlen`; `bar`; `mit Karte`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `ich möchte`, `bitte` (L13); prices (L14); `essen`, `trinken`, `gern` (L15); `können` (L11).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Record the ordered item, drink, and payment method from one complete café exchange.

### Lesson 18 — Checkpoint: Food Mission

- **Module:** 3 — Food, drink, and simple transactions
- **Real-life situation:** Buying groceries and then ordering one item at a café.
- **Can do:** I can complete two linked, predictable food transactions.

- **Deliberately recycled:** bakery food and `möchten` (L13); price and quantity language (L14); food preferences (L15); shopping-list items and `kein` (L16); café payment (L17).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Use a list and menu to buy two groceries, ask one price, order one drink, and close the transaction politely.

## Module 4 — Home and neighbourhood

### Lesson 19 — My Home

- **Module:** 4 — Home and neighbourhood
- **Real-life situation:** Describing a home to a visitor or potential flatmate.
- **Can do:** I can name the main rooms and say what a home has.

- **Sentence functions practised:** Statements; W-question; short answer.
- **Target passage:** Home description; 4 sentences; about 42–52 words.

- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Wohnung`; `das Haus`; `das Zimmer`; `die Küche`; `das Bad`; `das Schlafzimmer`; `es gibt`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `wohnen`, `die Stadt` (L2); family words (L7); `haben`, `kein` (L16); `aber` (L12).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Choose the matching floor plan from a short four-sentence home description.

### Lesson 20 — Where Is the Table?

No lesson schedules two substantial new structures. Case is introduced through concrete noun phrases and service/location needs; the full two-way-preposition contrast and systematic adjective declension are deliberately deferred beyond this A1 blueprint.

### 4. Retrieval and spaced repetition

The design uses four review distances: immediate (next 1–2 lessons), short (3–6 lessons), medium (7–18 lessons), and end-of-level. Examples:

| Anchor | Immediate | Short | Medium/long | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and W-questions (L1–L2) | L3–L6 | L7–L8 | L27, L31, L37 | L48 |
| Time and arrangements (L10–L11) | L12 | L17–L18 | L25–L30, L40–L42, L45–L46 | L48 |
| Food transaction language (L13–L17) | L18 | L24–L25 | L32–L36, L43, L46–L47 | L48 |
| Location and directions (L19–L23) | L24 | L25–L30 | L34–L37, L45, L47 | L48 |
| Repair strategies (L3) | L4–L6 | L13, L21 | L38, L47 | Required in L48 |
| Modal verbs (L11/L28/L40) | L12/L30/L41–L42 | L17–L21/L36 | L43–L47 | L48 |

| Perfekt (L43) | Productive L44 | Receptive L47 | — | Optional, not required, in L48 |

Every sixth lesson is a retrieval checkpoint. Its five new chunks are low-load formulae and are not part of the assessed success criteria.

### 5. Listening, reading, speaking, and writing

- **Listening:** Every lesson begins with the Nochmal audio-first passage. Fourteen activities additionally isolate listening performance, culminating in the uninterrupted 66–76 word rehearsal in L47.
- **Reading:** Notices, profiles, forms, maps, timetables, instructions, messages, and invitations are explicitly practised. Visual support decreases while familiar-language support and rereading remain available.
- **Speaking:** Guided role-plays move from single adjacency pairs (L2) to short service exchanges, directions, arrangements, reasons, and the final four-minute supported interaction (L48).
- **Writing:** Production moves from form fields (L4/L8), to isolated and linked sentences (L12/L22), to short functional messages (L28/L41/L44/L46). Free connected prose beyond these supported messages is not required at A1.

### 7. Passage-length, connectedness, and stamina progression

| Stage | Lessons | Planned passage shape | Connectedness/stamina goal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Early A1.1 | 1–6 | 3–4 short sentences/turns; 25–42 words | Formulae, one question–answer pair, then reference and repair across turns |
| Early A1.2 | 7–12 | 3–4 sentences/turns; 30–45 words | One topic across a mini-sequence; `und/aber/dann`; time-first openings |
| Mid A1.1 | 13–18 | 4–5 sentences/turns; 40–58 words | Complete predictable transactions with follow-up questions and closing formulae |
| Mid A1.2 | 19–24 | 4–5 sentences/turns; 42–58 words | Spatial reference, pronouns, cause/result, and two-step directions |
| Mid-to-late A1 | 25–30 | 4–5 sentences/turns; 48–62 words | One trip across announcement/message/dialogue; first explicit five-part timeline |
| Late A1.1 | 31–36 | 4–5 sentences/turns; 50–65 words | Short instructions and linked service problems across varied settings |
| Late A1.2 | 37–42 | 5–6 sentences/turns; 55–70 words | Varied openings, pronoun chains, proposals, alternatives, and one-clause reasons |
| Late A1.3 | 43–48 | 5–6 sentences/turns; 60–80 words | Short messages/accounts, simple past reference, mixed text types, and uninterrupted listening |

The largest adjacent upper-bound increase is four words, while sentence count grows only after learners have handled the earlier word range. Passage length must be reduced during authoring whenever the communicative job can be completed naturally with fewer words.

### 8. Final A1 “can do” checkpoint

L48 is realistic only under A1 conditions: familiar content, slow and clear delivery, brief turns, visible role support, and permission to request repetition. Success means the learner can:

1. give name and one personal detail;
2. understand one concrete time/place detail;
3. ask one W-question and one yes/no question;
4. make one polite request in a predictable transaction or service setting;
5. respond with a short answer and connect one additional fact with `und`, `aber`, `dann`, or `denn`; and
6. self-initiate one repair request if needed.

Perfekt, adjective endings, unscripted extended speech, and independent handling of an unfamiliar problem are not pass criteria.

## Gaps, uncertainties, and decisions for review

1. **Blueprint size:** This proposal uses 48 lessons rather than the current corpus’s 37 A1 records. It is intentionally a new planning sequence, not a renumbering or migration plan. Decide later whether to replace, map, or selectively adapt existing staged records.
2. **Late-A1 Perfekt:** L43 introduces a tightly controlled high-frequency Perfekt for practical comprehension and a rehearsed message. CEFR A1 does not require past narration. A qualified German teacher should confirm whether this belongs in core A1, optional A1+, or the start of A2.
3. **Checkpoint vocabulary tension:** The requested 5–8-new-item field conflicts with checkpoints that introduce nothing new. This draft uses exactly five low-load formulaic chunks in each checkpoint and excludes them from success criteria. Approve this compromise or permit `none` in checkpoint lessons.
4. **Environmental breadth:** L22 gives only a dog-walking and balcony-plant context. Confirm whether broader animal/nature vocabulary is worth displacing higher-frequency service or interaction language at A1.
5. **Lexical attestation:** Topic coverage and representative items were checked against the Goethe reference, but the full proposed target list has not yet been normalized and machine-audited. Do this before passage authoring; do not silently replace unattested but useful chunks without review.
6. **Productive vocabulary total:** The blueprint deliberately prioritizes high-utility chunks and repeated use over claiming all roughly 650 Goethe entries actively. Exact receptive versus productive targets should be tagged in structured metadata during a later authoring phase.
7. **Case scope:** Location dative, transport dative, selected accusative forms, and a few pronouns appear in context. Systematic two-way-preposition contrasts, adjective-declension production, and broad pronoun paradigms are deferred to avoid stacking structures.
8. **Text/audio verification:** Sentence and word ranges are planning bands, not quotas. Actual texts still require word counts, sentence-by-sentence grammar audit, native-level review, exact audio agreement, and listening trials before publication.
9. **Inclusive variants:** Family, work, health, and housing lessons need multiple authored scenario variants so learners are not forced into one family structure, employment status, ability, or housing arrangement. The blueprint names situations, not required personal disclosure.
10. **Publication status:** Nothing in this document is publication-ready. No lesson should be exposed through the app until its exact passage, breakdowns, form-change explanations, audio, validation, tests, and visual inspection pass the project release gate.
