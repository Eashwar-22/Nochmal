# Nochmal CEFR A2 curriculum blueprint

> **Status: DRAFT — UNPUBLISHED PLANNING ARTIFACT.** This file is not imported by the app, does not define production lesson IDs, and does not authorize publication. No passage text, sentence annotations, breakdown cards, or audio is included. Every future lesson remains subject to the full sentence audit and publication gate in `docs/grammar-breakdown-rulebook.md` and `docs/lesson-format.md`.

## Scope and evidence

This blueprint proposes 72 A2 lessons in twelve modules. A2 numbering restarts at Lesson 1; in the complete Nochmal path these lessons follow the 48 A1 lessons and therefore occupy positions 49–120. If production IDs are later approved, the natural namespace is `a2-course-001` through `a2-course-072`, but this planning document does not create those IDs.

The sequence assumes successful completion of the current A1 blueprint. It does not reteach A1 from the beginning: it immediately retrieves familiar transactions, directions, modals, repair language, and controlled Perfekt, then expands them into connected accounts, reasons, comparisons, polite problem-solving, and routine formal communication.

Every module contains five focused lessons and one retrieval checkpoint. Each focused lesson has one main communicative outcome and no more than one substantial new structure. Lesson 6 in each module introduces no substantial grammar and only five low-load chunks; those chunks support the performance but are not checkpoint criteria.

Coverage inputs:

- The [CEFR global A2 descriptor](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) defines the outer outcome: understanding frequently used language in areas of immediate relevance, exchanging information in routine tasks, and describing background, surroundings, and immediate needs in simple terms.
- The Council of Europe [CEFR descriptor resources](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) inform the listening, reading, spoken interaction, spoken production, written interaction, and written production outcomes. They are coverage criteria, not a mandatory German grammar order.
- The Goethe-Institut [Goethe-Zertifikat A2 vocabulary list](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/hr/Goethe-Zertifikat_A2_Wortliste.pdf) supplies the adult A2 lexical boundary and topic inventory. It is a reference inventory, not a ready-made sequence.
- The Goethe-Institut [official A2 adult practice materials](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/prf/gzsd2/ub2.html) inform realistic listening, reading, writing, and speaking task shapes. This remains a general A2 curriculum rather than an exam-preparation course.

All lesson passages remain audio-first in the Nochmal learning loop. A2 audio may contain two speakers, ordinary hesitation, redundant detail, and one irrelevant fact, but it must remain clearly articulated and appropriate to familiar everyday situations. The performance activity below is the additional retrieval task after comprehension and breakdown work.

## Entry assumptions from A1

The learner can already handle short supported exchanges involving identity, family, routine, food, housing, directions, transport, health, services, work, leisure, plans, and simple past reference. The following A1 structures are available for recycling rather than reintroduction:

- present-tense verb-second statements, W-questions, yes/no questions, and time-first inversion;
- separable verbs and high-frequency modal constructions with `können`, `müssen`, and `wollen`;
- polite `möchten`, basic imperatives, `kein`, selected accusative and dative forms, and fixed-location phrases;
- common repair language, `denn`, `deshalb`, and short linked sequences; and
- a controlled Perfekt with high-frequency verbs.

## Module 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences

### Lesson 1 — The Day Did Not Go to Plan

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Telling a friend about several things that went wrong yesterday.
- **Can do:** I can follow and give a short chronological account of completed everyday actions.
- **Primary language structure:** Expanded spoken Perfekt with common regular, irregular, and separable verbs in auxiliary–participle brackets.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Past statements; chronological narration; confirmation questions; brief reactions.
- **Target passage:** Short personal account; 5 sentences; about 60–68 words.
- **Connectedness:** One person and one day are held together by `zuerst`, `dann`, `später`, and `am Ende`.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `plötzlich`; `vergessen`; `verpassen`; `zurückkommen`; `endlich`; `zum Glück`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** controlled Perfekt and `gestern` (A1 L43–L44); routine and times (A1 L9–L12); transport (A1 L25–L30); `zuerst`, `danach`, `zum Schluss` (A1 L30).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Put five completed actions in order and identify the one problem that changed the day.

### Lesson 2 — When I Was New Here

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Describing the background to one’s first weeks in a new city.
- **Can do:** I can understand and state simple past background facts about how things were.
- **Primary language structure:** Natural spoken Präteritum of `sein` and `haben`: `war`, `waren`, `hatte`, and `hatten`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Past description; contrast between then and now; short follow-up questions.
- **Target passage:** Personal recollection; 5 sentences; about 62–70 words.
- **Connectedness:** `damals` establishes the past frame, while repeated reference to the same person and place prevents a list of isolated facts.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `damals`; `fremd`; `nervös`; `allein`; `die Erfahrung`; `am Anfang`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** origin and residence (A1 L1–L2); home and neighbourhood (A1 L19–L24); work/course (A1 L37–L38); present-versus-past reference from L1.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Give four supported sentences contrasting the first week in a place with the situation now.

### Lesson 3 — Because the Bus Was Late

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Explaining why one arrived late for work or an appointment.
- **Can do:** I can give a clear reason for a familiar event.
- **Primary language structure:** Subordinate reason clauses with `weil` and the finite verb in final position.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Explanation; apology; W-question with `warum`; response and acknowledgement.
- **Target passage:** Short workplace dialogue; 5 turns; about 64–72 words.
- **Connectedness:** A question triggers a reason, and the reason explains an earlier event rather than introducing a new topic.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `der Grund`; `die Verspätung`; `zu spät`; `warten`; `passieren`; `Bescheid sagen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `warum` and `denn` (A1 L41–L42); bus and appointments (A1 L10, L25, L28); past events (L1–L2); apology and repair (A1 L3, L38).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Complete three short explanations by joining a familiar event to its reason with `weil`.

### Lesson 4 — That Is Why I Changed the Plan

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Explaining the consequence of a delay or closed service.
- **Can do:** I can connect a familiar cause to a practical result.
- **Primary language structure:** Main-clause result connector `deshalb` with verb-second inversion.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Cause and result; plan change; suggestion; acceptance.
- **Target passage:** Message plus reply; 5 sentences/turns; about 66–74 words.
- **Connectedness:** The first fact causes the second action; pronouns and time references keep both messages on one plan.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Folge`; `ändern`; `absagen`; `verschieben`; `die Lösung`; `rechtzeitig`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `deshalb` in weather/home contexts (A1 L22); appointments and changes (A1 L28); plans and reasons (A1 L40–L42); `weil` (L3).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Match three problems to their consequences and note which plan is finally accepted.

### Lesson 5 — It Worked Anyway

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Describing a day that ended well despite a problem.
- **Can do:** I can signal a contrast between a difficulty and an unexpected positive result.
- **Primary language structure:** Main-clause contrast connector `trotzdem` with verb-second inversion.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Past account; contrast; evaluation; brief emotional response.
- **Target passage:** Connected account; 5–6 sentences; about 68–76 words.
- **Connectedness:** One problem, attempted response, and outcome are linked through `aber`, `deshalb`, and `trotzdem` without adding another storyline.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `trotzdem`; `klappen`; `schaffen`; `unerwartet`; `zufrieden`; `gelingen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** problem/result language (L3–L4); `am Ende` and `alles gut` (A1 L47); expanded Perfekt (L1); time and travel changes (A1 L26–L30).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Choose the accurate three-part summary: problem, response, and final result.

### Lesson 6 — Checkpoint: Explain What Happened

- **Module:** 1 — Past events, reasons, and consequences
- **Real-life situation:** Explaining a late arrival and agreeing on the next practical step.
- **Can do:** I can give a short connected account, provide a reason, and state the consequence.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of expanded Perfekt, `war/hatte`, `weil`, `deshalb`, and `trotzdem`; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Past narration; reason; result; contrast; apology; follow-up question.
- **Target passage:** Voicemail plus short dialogue; 6 sentences/turns; about 72–80 words.
- **Connectedness:** A recorded account supplies the facts used in the follow-up exchange, requiring information to be retained across two text types.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Was ist passiert?`; `Der Grund war …`; `Danach …`; `Deshalb …`; `Zum Glück …`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 1 structures and vocabulary; A1 repair, transport, appointment, and message language.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Give a 60–90 second supported account containing three past actions, one `weil` reason, and one result or contrast.

## Module 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison

### Lesson 7 — Which Apartment Is Better?

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Comparing two apartment advertisements.
- **Can do:** I can compare familiar features and say which option suits me better.
- **Primary language structure:** Comparative adjectives and adverbs with `-er` and `als`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Comparison; preference; reason; follow-up question.
- **Target passage:** Apartment discussion; 5–6 turns; about 66–74 words.
- **Connectedness:** Both speakers repeatedly compare the same two apartments across price, size, location, and noise.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `größer`; `kleiner`; `ruhiger`; `näher`; `die Miete`; `die Nebenkosten`; `vergleichen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** home and rooms (A1 L19–L21); price language (A1 L14); preferences (A1 L15, L39–L42); reason clauses (L3).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Compare two simple listings and give two reasons for choosing one.

### Lesson 8 — The Best Area for Us

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Choosing among three neighbourhoods for a move.
- **Can do:** I can identify the highest or lowest degree of a familiar quality.
- **Primary language structure:** Predicative/adverbial superlative with `am …sten` and common irregular forms such as `am besten`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Ranking; preference; agreement and disagreement; reason.
- **Target passage:** Family or shared-flat discussion; 5–6 turns; about 68–76 words.
- **Connectedness:** Three options are ranked using one stable set of criteria rather than described independently.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `am wichtigsten`; `am günstigsten`; `am ruhigsten`; `zentral`; `der Stadtteil`; `die Gegend`; `die Verbindung`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** comparisons (L7); neighbourhood places and transport (A1 L23–L26); plans and reasons (A1 L40–L42, L3–L4).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Complete a three-option table and identify the final choice and decisive criterion.

### Lesson 9 — A Bright Room with a Small Balcony

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Describing the apartment one is seeking to an agent or landlord.
- **Can do:** I can describe desired housing features with simple noun phrases.
- **Primary language structure:** Mixed adjective endings in nominative and accusative noun phrases after `ein`, `kein`, and possessive determiners.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Description; request; clarification; availability question.
- **Target passage:** Housing enquiry; 5 turns; about 68–78 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every description contributes to one requested apartment profile and is checked by the agent.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `hell`; `dunkel`; `modern`; `möbliert`; `der Balkon`; `die Fläche`; `der Keller`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** accusative noun phrases (A1 L13–L18, L33); apartment vocabulary (A1 L19–L21); comparisons (L7–L8); formal questions (A1 L23, L27).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Produce a four-sentence housing request containing one nominative and three accusative adjective+noun phrases.

### Lesson 10 — Where Should the Sofa Go?

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Deciding where furniture should be placed in a room.
- **Can do:** I can distinguish a fixed location from a destination or placement.
- **Primary language structure:** Two-way prepositions with `wo?` + dative and `wohin?` + accusative.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Location question; destination question; suggestion; correction.
- **Target passage:** Room-planning dialogue; 5–6 turns; about 70–78 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each suggestion changes or confirms the same room plan, so case choice follows a visible communicative contrast.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `wohin`; `das Sofa`; `die Wand`; `vor`; `zwischen`; `gegenüber`; `in die Ecke`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** fixed-location dative (A1 L20, L24); rooms and furniture (A1 L19–L21); directions (A1 L23); requests and confirmation.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Place four furniture icons on a room plan, distinguishing where an item is from where it is moved.

### Lesson 11 — Moving Day

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Giving and following simple instructions while moving furniture.
- **Can do:** I can describe putting objects somewhere and where they are afterward.
- **Primary language structure:** Placement/state verb contrasts: `stellen/stehen`, `legen/liegen`, and transitive/intransitive `hängen`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Instruction; location report; correction; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Moving-day instructions; 6 sentences/turns; about 72–80 words.
- **Connectedness:** The passage follows three objects from box or doorway to their final positions.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `der Umzug`; `der Karton`; `auspacken`; `aufbauen`; `das Regal`; `der Platz`; `fertig sein`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** two-way prepositions (L10); furniture and rooms (A1 L19–L21); imperatives (A1 L23, L32, L34); sequence markers (A1 L30, L1).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Direct a partner to place three pictured objects, then report where each object is.

### Lesson 12 — Checkpoint: Choose and Arrange a Home

- **Module:** 2 — Housing, moving, and comparison
- **Real-life situation:** Selecting an apartment and agreeing on a simple furniture plan.
- **Can do:** I can compare housing options, describe requirements, and discuss where things should go.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of comparative/superlative forms, selected adjective endings, two-way prepositions, and placement verbs; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Comparison; preference; reason; request; location/destination questions; agreement.
- **Target passage:** Listing plus viewing dialogue; 6 sentences/turns; about 76–84 words.
- **Connectedness:** Details from the written listing must be combined with information from the viewing conversation to make one choice.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Das passt besser.`; `Was ist Ihnen wichtig?`; `Hier ist genug Platz.`; `Stellen wir es dort hin.`; `So ist es gut.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 2 language; A1 home, neighbourhood, price, directions, and plan-making language.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Compare two supported listings, choose one with two reasons, and agree on the placement of three objects.

## Module 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help

### Lesson 13 — Could You Be Quieter?

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Politely asking a neighbour to reduce noise.
- **Can do:** I can make a polite request in a mildly difficult everyday situation.
- **Primary language structure:** Polite Konjunktiv II request with `könnten` plus infinitive.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Greeting; problem statement; polite request; response; agreement.
- **Target passage:** Doorstep dialogue; 5–6 turns; about 70–78 words.
- **Connectedness:** The request refers to a stated problem, and the response proposes a specific behavioural change.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `könnten`; `stören`; `laut`; `leise`; `die Ruhe`; `Rücksicht nehmen`; `die Musik`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** household problems and help (A1 L21); neighbours/home (A1 L19–L24); formal politeness and modals; reasons (L3).
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Make two polite neighbour requests from problem cards and respond constructively.

### Lesson 14 — I’m Sorry About the Noise

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Apologising, acknowledging another person’s concern, and promising a change.
- **Can do:** I can apologise for a specific action and respond to an apology.
- **Primary language structure:** Accusative reflexive pronouns with common reflexive verbs such as `sich entschuldigen` and `sich ärgern`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Apology; explanation; emotional reaction; promise; acceptance.
- **Target passage:** Follow-up neighbour conversation; 5–6 turns; about 72–80 words.
- **Connectedness:** Pronouns consistently refer to the same two neighbours and the same noise event.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `sich entschuldigen`; `sich ärgern`; `der Lärm`; `sich beruhigen`; `versprechen`; `unangenehm`; `kein Problem`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** polite requests (L13); past event and reason language (L1–L5); `mich/dich` (A1 L46); apology/repair chunks.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Identify what happened, how each person felt, and what change is promised.

### Lesson 15 — Do You Know Whether the Laundry Room Is Free?

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Asking a neighbour for information that may be uncertain.
- **Can do:** I can ask indirectly whether a simple fact is true.
- **Primary language structure:** Indirect yes/no questions with `ob` and final verb position.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Indirect question; uncertainty; answer; offer of help.
- **Target passage:** Hallway dialogue; 5 turns; about 72–80 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each turn narrows one practical question about access and timing.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `ob`; `wissen`; `die Waschküche`; `frei sein`; `die Hausordnung`; `der Hausmeister`; `nachsehen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** yes/no questions and time (A1 L4, L10–L12); home and services (A1 L19–L24); polite forms (L13); subordinate word order (L3).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Turn three direct yes/no questions into short indirect enquiries beginning with `Wissen Sie …?`.

### Lesson 16 — I Can Help Him

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Offering practical help to different people in the building.
- **Can do:** I can understand and use common dative pronouns for the person receiving help.
- **Primary language structure:** Dative personal pronouns `mir`, `dir`, `ihm`, `ihr`, `uns`, `euch`, and `Ihnen` with common verbs.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Offer; request; acceptance; report about another person.
- **Target passage:** Short multi-person exchange; 6 turns; about 74–82 words.
- **Connectedness:** The same task passes between speakers, making each pronoun’s referent recoverable from context.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `leihen`; `gehören`; `bringen`; `das Werkzeug`; `die Leiter`; `der Eimer`; `jemandem helfen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** dative `mir` (A1 L31–L32); help/service language (A1 L21, L35–L36); people and possessives; neighbour problems (L13–L15).
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Match each offered object or action to the person who receives it.

### Lesson 17 — Give Her the Package, Please

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Passing objects and messages to neighbours who are not home.
- **Can do:** I can state who receives something and what is transferred.
- **Primary language structure:** Dative recipient before an accusative noun object in common transfer patterns such as `jemandem etwas geben`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Request; transfer; message relay; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Delivery exchange; 5–6 turns; about 74–84 words.
- **Connectedness:** One package and one message are tracked across speaker, temporary receiver, and final recipient.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `abgeben`; `annehmen`; `weitergeben`; `der Zusteller`; `die Lieferung`; `unterschreiben`; `erreichen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** dative pronouns (L16); post and parcel language (A1 L34); messages/calls (A1 L44); formal imperatives and confirmation.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Relay two items and one message using picture cards for sender, recipient, and object.

### Lesson 18 — Checkpoint: Solve a Neighbour Problem

- **Module:** 3 — Neighbours, requests, and mutual help
- **Real-life situation:** Raising a building problem, asking for information, and arranging mutual help.
- **Can do:** I can handle a polite neighbour exchange and keep track of who needs or receives what.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of `könnten`, reflexive verbs, indirect `ob` questions, dative pronouns, and recipient-object patterns; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Complaint; apology; indirect question; offer; request; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Three-speaker building dialogue; 6–7 turns; about 80–86 words.
- **Connectedness:** A noise problem and a delivery question converge in one shared practical solution.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Darf ich kurz stören?`; `Das tut mir leid.`; `Ich sehe nach.`; `Ich kümmere mich darum.`; `Danke für Ihr Verständnis.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 3 language; A1 repair, household problem, post, time, and help language.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Complete a two-minute role-play containing one polite request, one apology, one indirect question, and one offer of help.

## Module 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication

### Lesson 19 — An Interesting Position

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Reading a job advertisement and deciding whether to apply.
- **Can do:** I can understand the main requirements in a simple job advertisement and state my interest.
- **Primary language structure:** Reflexive verb with fixed preposition: `sich um eine Stelle bewerben`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Description of requirements; personal evaluation; reason; intention.
- **Target passage:** Job advertisement plus short conversation; 5–6 sentences/turns; about 72–82 words.
- **Connectedness:** Advertisement details are repeated selectively in the conversation as reasons for or against applying.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Stelle`; `sich bewerben`; `die Bewerbung`; `der Beruf`; `die Voraussetzung`; `der Lebenslauf`; `die Anzeige`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** work and tasks (A1 L37–L38); personal experience (L2); internet and reading (A1 L39); reasons (L3–L5); reflexive pronouns (L14).
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Mark required experience, hours, and contact method, then decide whether a short applicant profile fits.

### Lesson 20 — What I Did Before

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Giving a simple chronological account of education and work experience.
- **Can do:** I can describe two or three completed stages of my background.
- **Primary language structure:** Past-time expressions with `vor` plus dative and a recycled Perfekt sequence.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Background account; dates and duration; clarification question.
- **Target passage:** Short interview answer; 5–6 sentences; about 74–82 words.
- **Connectedness:** Education, practical training, and employment form one ordered biography rather than unrelated facts.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Ausbildung`; `das Praktikum`; `der Abschluss`; `die Tätigkeit`; `vor einem Jahr`; `die Qualifikation`; `Berufserfahrung haben`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** personal dates (A1 L8); work/course language (A1 L37–L38); expanded Perfekt (L1); sequence markers.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Use a three-point timeline to give a one-minute account of an invented applicant’s background.

### Lesson 21 — The First Day at Work

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Telling someone what happened during workplace orientation.
- **Can do:** I can follow an account containing common inseparable past participles.
- **Primary language structure:** Perfekt with common inseparable verbs, especially `be-`, `er-`, and `ver-` verbs without `ge-`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Past narration; workplace report; evaluation; follow-up question.
- **Target passage:** First-day account; 6 sentences; about 76–84 words.
- **Connectedness:** One employee moves through arrival, introductions, explanation, first task, and closing feedback.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Abteilung`; `die Firma`; `sich vorstellen`; `die Arbeitszeit`; `bekommen`; `die Verantwortung`; `die Einführung`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** workplace people/tasks (A1 L37–L38); reflexive verbs (L14, L19); Perfekt (L1, L20); time sequencing.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Identify four orientation steps and distinguish which actions happened from which facts merely describe the workplace.

### Lesson 22 — I Think That the Team Is Friendly

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Giving a simple opinion about a new workplace.
- **Can do:** I can report a thought, belief, or hope in a complete sentence.
- **Primary language structure:** Complement clauses with `dass` and finite verb in final position.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Opinion; belief; hope; agreement; gentle disagreement.
- **Target passage:** Colleague conversation; 5–6 turns; about 76–84 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each opinion refers back to the same team, work schedule, or task introduced at the start.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `glauben`; `denken`; `hoffen`; `sicher`; `das Team`; `die Zusammenarbeit`; `zuverlässig`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** workplace orientation (L19–L21); adjectives and comparisons (L7–L9); subordinate word order with `weil` and `ob` (L3, L15).
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Complete a short workplace message with three `dass` clauses expressing opinion, belief, and hope.

### Lesson 23 — I Would Like to Introduce Myself

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Writing a polite email before an interview or first meeting.
- **Can do:** I can express a polite wish and provide essential availability information.

- **Sentence functions practised:** Formal opening; polite intention; availability; request for confirmation; formal closing.
- **Target passage:** Short formal email; 5–6 sentences; about 78–86 words.

- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `ich würde gern`; `erreichbar`; `die Rückmeldung`; `das Gespräch`; `zur Verfügung stehen`; `Sehr geehrte …`; `Mit freundlichen Grüßen`.

### Lesson 24 — Checkpoint: From Advertisement to First Day

- **Module:** 4 — Work, applications, and workplace communication
- **Real-life situation:** Understanding a vacancy, answering interview questions, and reporting on a first day.
- **Can do:** I can exchange routine information about qualifications, availability, and workplace experience.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of application language, past background, inseparable participles, `dass`, and `würde gern`; no new substantial structure.

### 5. Listening, reading, speaking, and writing

- **Listening:** Every lesson begins with an audio-first passage. Detail tasks progress from ordered personal accounts to linked announcements, messages, and two-speaker problem-solving. L71 reaches 102–114 words, but remains clearly articulated and familiar in content.
- **Reading:** Learners work with advertisements, forms, notices, timetables, instructions, emails, complaints, programmes, forecasts, and short administrative documents. Tasks require locating, comparing, and combining details rather than translating every word.
- **Speaking:** Guided interaction develops from a one-minute past account to comparisons, polite complaints, indirect questions, advice, event planning, opinions, formal problem reports, and the five- to six-minute supported scenario in L72.
- **Writing:** Production moves from connected explanations and formal emails to complaints, plans, recommendations, form-related messages, deadline requests, and an 80-word reflection. Models and communicative prompts remain available; unrestricted essay writing is not an A2 requirement.
- **Mediation/relay:** Learners repeatedly pass practical information from doctor to family member, announcement to travel partner, advertisement to applicant, notice to group, or service call to written follow-up.

Passage ranges are planning ceilings, not quotas. Authoring should use the shortest natural passage that performs the communicative job. Longer passages must gain coherence and information value, not filler or unnecessary subordinate clauses.

### 8. Final A2 “can do” checkpoint

L72 is realistic only under A2 conditions: familiar domains, clearly articulated standard speech, short connected turns, visible scenario support, and permission to ask for clarification. Success means the learner can:

1. understand the main situation and retrieve several concrete details from linked familiar inputs;
2. give a short connected account of what happened using familiar past forms and temporal links;
3. provide a reason, consequence, contrast, or condition;
4. ask at least two indirect questions to obtain missing information;
5. compare two practical options and express a simple opinion;
6. make a polite request and negotiate an acceptable next step;
7. confirm or relay the agreed plan; and
8. notice and repair one misunderstanding rather than continuing on a false assumption.

Abstract debate, spontaneous extended storytelling, nuanced register control, broad passive constructions, and detailed argumentation are not pass criteria. Those belong to later development.

## Gaps, uncertainties, and decisions for review

1. **Blueprint size:** Seventy-two lessons is an internal completeness decision, not a CEFR requirement. It provides twelve retrieval checkpoints and enough spacing for structures to recur across domains.
2. **Numbering:** This file uses A2 Lesson 1–72. The combined path position is A1 1–48 followed by A2 1–72. Production IDs remain undecided until implementation begins.
3. **Legacy curriculum:** An older 80-lesson A1–A2 corpus exists in Git history and was used only as a topic cross-check. This blueprint does not restore, renumber, or authorize those deleted records; useful scenarios may be adapted later through the normal candidate workflow.
4. **A1-to-A2 Perfekt bridge:** The first module assumes the controlled Perfekt from A1 L43–L48 is receptive and partly productive. If learner data shows otherwise, add review inside L1 rather than creating hidden prerequisites.
5. **Grammar boundary:** Relative clauses, genitive after `wegen`, `werden` future, and the `als/wenn` contrast can be placed differently across German programmes. They are scheduled here because they serve concrete A2 functions, not because CEFR mandates them. A qualified German-language reviewer should confirm their productive scope.
6. **Adjective scope:** The blueprint focuses productive work on common determiner-led nominative, accusative, and dative patterns. Strong no-determiner patterns remain mainly receptive. Learners do not need to recite tables, though every authored form still needs correct typed inflection help.
7. **Präteritum scope:** Productive Präteritum is restricted mainly to `sein`, `haben`, and common modal verbs. Spoken event narration continues to prefer Perfekt.
8. **Lexical attestation:** The proposed focus targets have not yet been normalized and machine-audited against the official A2 wordlist. Complete that audit before passage authoring and review any unattested but high-utility chunks explicitly.
9. **Receptive versus productive load:** Some public-information, insurance, and administrative vocabulary should probably remain receptive. Mark that distinction in structured metadata rather than requiring every listed noun in free production.
10. **Listening authenticity:** Clear delivery does not mean unnaturally segmented speech. Actual audio should be tested for pace, number density, name clarity, and whether irrelevant detail makes the task unfair.
11. **Compiler readiness:** Before an A2 candidate uses a new structure, confirm that controlled tags, verb tables, inflection paradigms, phrase annotations, validation, and rendering support it. Extend reusable models and tests first; never add lesson-sentence branches.
12. **Inclusive scenarios:** Housing, family, employment, health, ability, finances, and personal history require varied authored scenarios. No checkpoint should require a learner to disclose sensitive real information.
13. **Exam relationship:** Goethe task shapes inform variety and realism, but this sequence is not an exam-preparation course and should not train answer patterns at the expense of everyday communication.
14. **Publication status:** Nothing in this document is publication-ready. Every exact passage must still receive a sentence-by-sentence audit, typed cards and form-change help, exact translation/audio agreement, validation, tests, and visual inspection before it can enter any published-lesson list.
