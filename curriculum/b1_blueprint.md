# Nochmal CEFR B1 curriculum blueprint

> **Status: DRAFT — UNPUBLISHED PLANNING ARTIFACT.** This file is not imported by the app, does not define production lesson IDs, and does not authorize publication. It contains no final passages, translations, sentence annotations, breakdown cards, or audio. Every future lesson remains hidden until it passes the complete sentence audit and publication gate in `docs/grammar-breakdown-rulebook.md` and `docs/lesson-format.md`.

## Scope and evidence

This blueprint proposes **96 B1 lessons in sixteen modules**. B1 numbering restarts at Lesson 1; in the complete Nochmal path these lessons follow 48 A1 and 72 A2 lessons, so they occupy positions **121–216**. If production IDs are later approved, the natural namespace is `b1-course-001` through `b1-course-096`; this planning file does not create or publish those IDs.

The sequence assumes successful completion of `curriculum/a2_blueprint.md`. It does not restart A2. Familiar past narration, reasons, comparisons, polite requests, indirect questions, relative clauses, conditions, formal messages, and everyday problem-solving become retrieval material. B1 adds longer connected discourse, clearer chronology, hypotheses, passive processes, richer relative reference, structured opinion, source relay, collaboration, and independent handling of familiar complications.

Every module contains five focused lessons and one checkpoint. A focused lesson has one principal communicative outcome and no more than one substantial new structure. Every sixth lesson introduces no substantial grammar and only five low-load chunks; those chunks support performance but are not checkpoint criteria.

Coverage inputs:

- The Council of Europe [CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4) supplies the B1 outcome boundary: connected linear discourse, clear chronology, main points and important details in clear standard-language input, practical interaction, reasons for views, and relay of straightforward information.
- The Council of Europe [CEFR descriptor resources](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) inform reception, production, interaction, online interaction, and mediation coverage. CEFR does not prescribe a German grammar sequence.
- The Goethe-Institut [official B1 vocabulary list and adult practice materials](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/prf/ueb/pb1.html) provide an adult lexical and task-shape cross-check across listening, reading, writing, and speaking. This remains a general B1 curriculum, not an exam-preparation course.

All passages remain audio-first. Early B1 audio should use clear standard German with natural linking and limited hesitation. Later passages may include two or three speakers, ordinary redundancy, paraphrase, competing details, and one or two irrelevant facts, but not dense idiom, strong regional speech, or rapid unsupported debate. Performance activities follow comprehension and breakdown study.

## Entry assumptions from A2

The learner can already manage routine and mildly disrupted situations involving housing, work, health, travel, services, events, learning, community life, and administration. The following are available for recycling rather than first introduction:

- connected Perfekt, common Präteritum of `sein`, `haben`, and modals, plus `als/wenn` for basic past reference;
- `weil`, `dass`, `ob`, indirect W-questions, `wenn`, `bevor`, `deshalb`, `trotzdem`, and future `werden`;
- comparative and superlative forms, determiner-led adjective endings, two-way prepositions, placement verbs, and nominative/accusative relative clauses;
- polite `könnten/würden` chunks, reflexive verbs, dative recipients and pronouns, common verb-preposition combinations, `zu`-infinitives, and `um … zu`; and
- short formal requests, complaints, opinions, recommendations, plans, and source-to-person relay.

## Module 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology

### Lesson 1 — Tell the Whole Story

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Giving a colleague a coherent account of an eventful day.
- **Can do:** I can distinguish background, state, and completed events in a connected spoken account.
- **Primary language structure:** Narrative tense selection: Perfekt for completed spoken events and familiar Präteritum forms for background with `sein`, `haben`, and modals.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Narration; background description; chronology; follow-up question; evaluation.
- **Target passage:** Personal account; 7–8 sentences; about 104–116 words.
- **Connectedness:** One speaker, day, problem, and outcome are maintained through temporal markers and repeated reference.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `der Ablauf`; `zunächst`; `inzwischen`; `unerwartet`; `schließlich`; `im Nachhinein`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** A2 past forms, sequence markers, reasons/results, workplace and transport language.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Reconstruct the event line and label each detail as background, event, or result.

### Lesson 2 — It Had Already Happened

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Explaining why a later problem could no longer be prevented.
- **Can do:** I can mark an event that happened before another past event.
- **Primary language structure:** Plusquamperfekt with `hatte/war` plus past participle in short background statements.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Earlier-past background; later event; cause; realization; clarification.
- **Target passage:** Problem account; 7–8 sentences; about 106–118 words.
- **Connectedness:** Two past layers explain one consequence; explicit time anchors prevent tense switching from becoming decorative.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `bereits`; `vorher`; `bemerken`; `übersehen`; `zu spät sein`; `nicht mehr ändern können`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Perfekt, `war/hatte`, participles, transport/service problems, `deshalb` and `trotzdem`.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Combine four event pairs into a six-sentence account that makes the earlier action unambiguous.

### Lesson 3 — After the Meeting Had Ended

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Reporting the sequence around a meeting and a missed decision.
- **Can do:** I can connect two past events and show which one was completed first.
- **Primary language structure:** Temporal subordinate clauses with `nachdem`, normally pairing an earlier Plusquamperfekt event with a later past event.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Sequencing; reporting; explanation; checking chronology.
- **Target passage:** Workplace account; 7–8 sentences/turns; about 108–120 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every clause contributes to one meeting timeline and its practical consequence.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `nachdem`; `die Besprechung`; `eine Entscheidung treffen`; `die Unterlagen`; `weiterleiten`; `missverstehen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Plusquamperfekt (L2), clause-final verbs, workplace scheduling, indirect questions.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Retell five pictured events with one `nachdem` clause and correct earlier/later ordering.

### Lesson 4 — Although the Start Was Difficult

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Describing a difficult start that led to a positive result.
- **Can do:** I can express a concession inside a connected account.
- **Primary language structure:** Concessive subordinate clauses with `obwohl` and verb-final order.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Difficulty; concession; unexpected result; personal evaluation.
- **Target passage:** Experience narrative; 7–8 sentences; about 110–122 words.
- **Connectedness:** The difficulty and achievement remain logically opposed throughout the account.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `obwohl`; `die Herausforderung`; `aufgeben`; `durchhalten`; `sich lohnen`; `stolz auf`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `trotzdem`, past narration, emotional reactions, `sich` plus preposition.
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Match four challenges to outcomes and explain two contrasts with `obwohl`.

### Lesson 5 — While Everything Was Changing

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Describing simultaneous developments during a move or job change.
- **Can do:** I can show that two events or situations were happening at the same time.
- **Primary language structure:** Temporal `während` clauses with verb-final order.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Simultaneity; contrastive observation; narration; summary.
- **Target passage:** Personal transition account; 8 sentences; about 112–124 words.
- **Connectedness:** Two simultaneous strands belong to one life change and rejoin in the conclusion.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `während`; `gleichzeitig`; `sich gewöhnen an`; `die Veränderung`; `nebenbei`; `allmählich`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** past tense selection, reflexive verbs, housing/work change, `obwohl`.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Complete a two-column timeline and identify which developments overlapped.

### Lesson 6 — Checkpoint: Reconstruct an Unexpected Day

- **Module:** 1 — Connected stories and clear chronology
- **Real-life situation:** Reconstructing a disrupted day from a voicemail and a follow-up conversation.
- **Can do:** I can understand and retell a connected story with clear time relationships and contrast.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of narrative tense selection, Plusquamperfekt, `nachdem`, `obwohl`, and `während`; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Narration; chronology; background; cause; concession; clarification; result.
- **Target passage:** Voicemail plus dialogue; 8–9 sentences/turns; about 116–130 words.
- **Connectedness:** Information from both inputs is required to build one accurate event line.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Wenn ich es richtig verstanden habe …`; `Das war vorher.`; `Zur gleichen Zeit …`; `Trotz allem …`; `So ist es dazu gekommen.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 1 language plus A2 repair, messages, workplace, transport, and service vocabulary.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Give a 90-second retelling with background, three events, one earlier-past event, one overlap, and one concession.

## Module 2 — Personal history and precise description

### Lesson 7 — Since Then, a Lot Has Changed

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Explaining how a past decision continues to affect the present.
- **Can do:** I can connect a past starting event to its continuing consequence.
- **Primary language structure:** `seitdem` as a temporal conjunction and linking adverb.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Change over time; continuing state; cause; present evaluation.
- **Target passage:** Personal reflection; 8 sentences; about 112–124 words.
- **Connectedness:** One turning point links the past and present; repeated time reference sustains the development.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `seitdem`; `der Wendepunkt`; `sich entwickeln`; `mittlerweile`; `dauerhaft`; `eine Rolle spielen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `seit` plus present, past narration, future plans, `nachdem`.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Produce a 90-word before–turning point–since then reflection.

### Lesson 8 — The Person I Learned From

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Describing a person who offered important help or advice.
- **Can do:** I can identify a person referred to through a dative relationship.
- **Primary language structure:** Relative clauses with dative relative pronouns `dem`, `der`, and `denen`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Description; gratitude; recipient relation; example; explanation.
- **Target passage:** Short portrait; 8 sentences; about 114–126 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every detail identifies the same person and explains the speaker’s appreciation.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `unterstützen`; `vertrauen`; `der Rat`; `dankbar`; `zuverlässig`; `ein Vorbild sein`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** dative pronouns, relative-clause order, personal history, work and learning language.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Describe two people using one dative relative clause for each.

### Lesson 9 — The Project I Am Proud Of

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Presenting a project or experience that matters personally.
- **Can do:** I can connect a noun to a verb or adjective that requires a preposition.
- **Primary language structure:** Relative clauses with a preposition plus relative pronoun, such as `auf das`, `mit dem`, and `für die`.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Identification; description; evaluation; reason; follow-up.
- **Target passage:** Informal presentation; 8–9 sentences; about 116–130 words.
- **Connectedness:** The project is introduced, specified, evaluated, and connected to later plans.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `das Ergebnis`; `beteiligt sein`; `Verantwortung übernehmen`; `präsentieren`; `gelungen`; `Erfahrung sammeln`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** common prepositional verbs, relative clauses, adjective endings, learning/work vocabulary.
- **Performance activity:** **Reading and speaking:** Identify each relative-clause referent, then give a four-sentence project portrait.

### Lesson 10 — Fresh Bread and Warm Soup

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Describing sensory details at a market, celebration, or remembered place.
- **Can do:** I can use common adjective+noun phrases without an article.
- **Primary language structure:** Strong adjective endings in high-frequency nominative, accusative, and dative phrases without a determiner.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Description; preference; recommendation; memory.
- **Target passage:** Descriptive recollection; 8 sentences; about 118–132 words.
- **Connectedness:** Sensory details build one setting rather than an inventory of unrelated objects.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `frisch`; `hausgemacht`; `der Duft`; `würzig`; `regionales Essen`; `mit kaltem Wasser`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** determiner-led adjective endings, food/events, local culture, past description.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Create a coherent six-sentence place description using four audited no-determiner adjective phrases.

### Lesson 11 — My Colleague’s Recommendation

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Referring precisely to people’s roles, possessions, and recommendations.
- **Can do:** I can understand and use common possessive genitive noun phrases.
- **Primary language structure:** Possessive genitive attributes after a head noun and possessive proper-name constructions, with productive scope limited to common transparent patterns.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Possession; attribution; source identification; explanation.
- **Target passage:** Recommendation account; 8–9 sentences; about 120–134 words.
- **Connectedness:** Advice, source, and result remain tied to one decision.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `die Empfehlung`; `der Vorschlag`; `der Einfluss`; `die Meinung`; `jemandes Erfahrung`; `überzeugen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** possessive determiners, reasons, reported content with `dass`, relative reference.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Identify who owns, recommends, or is responsible for six referenced items.

### Lesson 12 — Checkpoint: Present a Turning Point

- **Module:** 2 — Personal history and precise description
- **Real-life situation:** Presenting a person, place, or project connected to an important change.
- **Can do:** I can give a precise connected description and explain its continuing importance.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of `seitdem`, dative and prepositional relative clauses, strong adjective endings, and possessive genitive; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Description; chronology; attribution; gratitude; evaluation; follow-up.
- **Target passage:** Profile plus interview; 9 sentences/turns; about 122–138 words.
- **Connectedness:** A profile supplies details reused in the interview and final reflection.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Was mich besonders geprägt hat …`; `Seit diesem Zeitpunkt …`; `Dabei handelt es sich um …`; `Daran erinnere ich mich gut.`; `Das bedeutet mir viel.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 2 language plus Module 1 narration and A2 biography, work, culture, and learning vocabulary.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Give a two-minute supported presentation with a turning point, precise person/object reference, three descriptive noun phrases, and its present effect.

## Module 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions

### Lesson 13 — If Only I Had More Time

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Talking about a present wish that does not match reality.
- **Can do:** I can express unreal present wishes with appropriate distance from fact.
- **Primary language structure:** Present-time Konjunktiv II wishes with `wäre`, `hätte`, and selected modal forms.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Wish; limitation; reason; empathy; follow-up.
- **Target passage:** Conversation about work-life balance; 8–9 turns; about 120–134 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each wish responds to the same time and responsibility problem.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `wenn ich doch …`; `mehr Zeit haben`; `flexibel`; `die Belastung`; `sich etwas wünschen`; `realistisch`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** polite Konjunktiv II chunks, work schedules, free time, reasons.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Separate facts from wishes and identify the obstacle behind each wish.

### Lesson 14 — I Would Choose the Smaller Team

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Comparing imagined outcomes before making a choice.
- **Can do:** I can say what I would do in a hypothetical situation.
- **Primary language structure:** `würde` plus infinitive for hypothetical actions.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Hypothesis; preference; comparison; consequence; justification.
- **Target passage:** Decision discussion; 8–9 turns; about 122–136 words.
- **Connectedness:** Two options are evaluated against the same criteria and imagined consequences.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `in diesem Fall`; `sich entscheiden für`; `das Risiko`; `die Möglichkeit`; `eher`; `unter diesen Umständen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** comparisons, future plans, reasons/results, `würden` requests.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Choose between two realistic options and state three hypothetical consequences.

### Lesson 15 — If I Worked Fewer Hours

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Discussing an unreal condition and its possible result.
- **Can do:** I can connect a hypothetical condition to an imagined consequence.
- **Primary language structure:** Unreal present `wenn` clauses with Konjunktiv II in condition and result.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Condition; consequence; speculation; doubt; response.
- **Target passage:** Work/study planning dialogue; 9 turns; about 124–138 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every proposed consequence traces back to one hypothetical schedule change.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `wenn das möglich wäre`; `sich leisten können`; `reduzieren`; `die Voraussetzung`; `zur Verfügung stehen`; `infrage kommen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** real `wenn`, `würde` (L14), `hätte/wäre` (L13), time and money language.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Build a coherent decision chain with two unreal conditions and three consequences.

### Lesson 16 — In Your Position, I Would Ask

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Giving respectful advice about a job, course, or housing decision.
- **Can do:** I can offer advice without presenting it as an order.
- **Primary language structure:** Advice frame `an deiner/Ihrer Stelle würde ich …` and related Konjunktiv II recommendations.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Advice; concern; alternative; acceptance/rejection; reason.
- **Target passage:** Advice conversation; 9 turns; about 126–140 words.
- **Connectedness:** Advice responds to stated constraints and is evaluated against them.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `an deiner Stelle`; `abwägen`; `sich beraten lassen`; `vernünftig`; `Bedenken haben`; `eine Chance nutzen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** hypothetical action, polite register, indirect questions, options and comparisons.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Respond to two problem cards with advice, reason, and alternative.

### Lesson 17 — Either Now or Next Month

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Narrowing a decision to two viable alternatives.
- **Can do:** I can present mutually exclusive choices clearly.
- **Primary language structure:** Paired conjunction `entweder … oder` in coordinated words and clauses.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Alternative; constraint; choice; negotiation; agreement.
- **Target passage:** Planning exchange; 9 turns; about 128–142 words.
- **Connectedness:** Time, cost, and availability all serve one final either/or decision.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `entweder … oder`; `eine Alternative`; `festlegen`; `verschieben`; `rechtzeitig`; `eine Entscheidung steht an`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** hypothetical conditions, scheduling, indirect enquiry, comparison.
- **Performance activity:** **Reading:** Trace three alternatives through a message thread and identify the final agreed choice.

### Lesson 18 — Checkpoint: Decide Under Constraints

- **Module:** 3 — Wishes, possibilities, and difficult decisions
- **Real-life situation:** Choosing between study, work, housing, or travel options with imperfect conditions.
- **Can do:** I can discuss wishes, hypothetical consequences, advice, and alternatives before reaching a decision.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of present Konjunktiv II, unreal `wenn`, advice frames, and `entweder … oder`; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Wish; hypothesis; advice; comparison; alternative; negotiation; decision.
- **Target passage:** Two-person planning conversation; 9–10 turns; about 132–148 words.
- **Connectedness:** A fixed set of constraints is revisited until the speakers reach one defensible plan.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Was wäre, wenn …?`; `Ich sehe zwei Möglichkeiten.`; `Das käme für mich infrage.`; `Ich würde eher …`; `Dann steht die Entscheidung fest.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 3 language plus finances, schedules, work, courses, housing, and transport.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Negotiate a two- to three-minute decision, state one wish, test two hypothetical outcomes, give advice, and choose an option with reasons.

## Module 4 — Work, responsibility, and professional development

### Lesson 19 — How the Orders Are Processed

- **Module:** 4 — Work, responsibility, and professional development
- **Real-life situation:** Understanding how a routine workplace process works.
- **Can do:** I can understand and describe a process when the action matters more than the actor.
- **Primary language structure:** Present event passive with `werden` plus past participle.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Process description; sequencing; responsibility question; clarification.

- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `bearbeiten`; `prüfen`; `weiterleiten`; `zuständig`; `der Vorgang`; `abschließen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** sequence markers, workplace roles, separable/inseparable participles, `man` descriptions.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** Put five process steps in order and distinguish actor information from process information.

### Lesson 20 — What Was Decided Yesterday

- **Module:** 4 — Work, responsibility, and professional development

- **Can do:** I can describe what was done in a past process without naming every actor.

### Lesson 21 — The Form Must Be Submitted Today

### Lesson 22 — We Are Working on It

- **Module:** 4 — Work, responsibility, and professional development
- **Real-life situation:** Updating a team about tasks, dependencies, and concerns.
- **Can do:** I can refer back naturally to things introduced with prepositional verbs.

### Lesson 23 — Not Only Faster, but Also Safer

- **Module:** 4 — Work, responsibility, and professional development

### Lesson 24 — Checkpoint: Explain a Workplace Change

- **Can do:** I can obtain information, understand the process, report facts, and write the necessary formal response.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of indirect questions, administrative passive, incident chronology, application cohesion, and formal hypothetical requests; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Enquiry; process; evidence; clarification; request; deadline; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Office notice, case message, and phone exchange; 11–12 sentences/turns; about 192–206 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every text updates the same case and changes the next required action.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Bezug nehmend auf …`; `In meinem Fall …`; `Als Nachweis sende ich …`; `Bitte teilen Sie mir mit …`; `Damit ist der Vorgang vollständig.`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 14 language plus source attribution, process maps, contingency, and repair.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing checkpoint:** Produce the final 150-word case response with reference number, verified facts, attached evidence, one focused request, and deadline confirmation.

## Module 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation

### Lesson 85 — My Position and My Reasons

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Giving a structured view on a familiar social, workplace, education, or local issue.
- **Can do:** I can state a position and support it with connected reasons and examples.
- **Primary language structure:** Simple argument structure: position, reason, example, qualification, conclusion.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Position; reason; example; qualification; conclusion.
- **Target passage:** Prepared contribution; 10–11 sentences; about 188–202 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each reason supports the same claim, and examples are clearly marked as evidence rather than new topics.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `meiner Ansicht nach`; `dafür gibt es mehrere Gründe`; `ein Beispiel dafür`; `allerdings`; `entscheidend ist`; `daraus folgt`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** reason/result, stance markers, balanced argument, presentation organisation.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Give a two-minute opinion with two reasons, one example, one limitation, and a conclusion.

### Lesson 86 — I Agree Only Partly

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Responding respectfully to a view one does not completely accept.
- **Can do:** I can show partial agreement, introduce a counterpoint, and keep the discussion constructive.
- **Primary language structure:** Concessive response frames and counter-argument cohesion; no new substantial grammar.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Agreement; partial agreement; disagreement; counterpoint; question.
- **Target passage:** Small-group discussion; 11–12 turns; about 190–204 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each response explicitly links to the preceding claim before adding a counterpoint.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Da stimme ich zu.`; `nur teilweise`; `Ich sehe das etwas anders.`; `Dabei darf man nicht vergessen …`; `Das überzeugt mich nicht ganz.`; `Wie begründest du das?`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** `zwar … aber`, `obwohl`, fact/opinion, reasons, turn management.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Respond to four claims using four different degrees of agreement and a relevant follow-up.

### Lesson 87 — Let Me Put That Another Way

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Clarifying an idea when the first explanation was not understood.
- **Can do:** I can paraphrase a familiar point, give a concrete example, and check understanding.
- **Primary language structure:** Clarification and compensatory strategies using paraphrase, definition, example, and contrast.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Reformulation; definition; example; contrast; comprehension check.
- **Target passage:** Discussion with repair; 11–12 turns; about 192–206 words.
- **Connectedness:** Every reformulation preserves the same intended meaning while changing form or concreteness.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `anders formuliert`; `damit meine ich`; `genauer gesagt`; `zum Beispiel`; `im Unterschied zu`; `Ist jetzt klar, was ich meine?`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** paraphrase (L64), relative definitions, repair language, source/reference forms.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Explain three blocked words or ideas without translation, then verify the partner’s understanding.

### Lesson 88 — What Both Sources Say

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Relaying the common and differing points from two short familiar sources.
- **Can do:** I can combine essential information from two sources while preserving differences and attribution.
- **Primary language structure:** Multi-source synthesis frames; no new substantial grammar.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Source; shared point; difference; uncertainty; practical implication.
- **Target passage:** Short notice and interview excerpt; 11–12 sentences/turns; about 194–208 words.
- **Connectedness:** The synthesis is organised by issue, not by mechanically retelling source one and then source two.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Beide Quellen nennen …`; `Übereinstimmend wird gesagt …`; `Unterschiedlich ist …`; `Während Quelle A …`; `nicht eindeutig`; `für die Entscheidung relevant`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** source attribution, contrastive `während`, fact/opinion, paraphrase, news language.
- **Performance activity:** **Mediation:** Give a 120-second synthesis with two agreements, one difference, one uncertainty, and one practical implication.

### Lesson 89 — A Compromise Everyone Can Accept

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Negotiating a solution after participants state different priorities.
- **Can do:** I can identify common ground, propose a compromise, and confirm responsibilities.
- **Primary language structure:** Negotiation and consensus frames; no new substantial grammar.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Priority; common ground; proposal; concession; agreement; assignment.
- **Target passage:** Three-person negotiation; 12 turns; about 196–210 words.
- **Connectedness:** Proposals are tested against all stated priorities until one solution remains.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `uns ist gemeinsam, dass …`; `entgegenkommen`; `ein Kompromiss wäre …`; `unter dieser Voraussetzung`; `damit können alle leben`; `verbindlich festlegen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** hypotheticals, paired conjunctions, conditions, collaboration, reciprocal language.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Mediate between two positions, summarize priorities, propose two compromises, and record the accepted one.

### Lesson 90 — Checkpoint: Discuss, Clarify, and Mediate

- **Module:** 15 — Opinions, discussion, and mediation
- **Real-life situation:** Helping a group decide a familiar issue from two sources and conflicting preferences.
- **Can do:** I can present a reasoned position, respond constructively, clarify meaning, synthesize sources, and support agreement.
- **Primary language structure:** Consolidation of argument, counter-response, paraphrase, source synthesis, and negotiation; no new substantial structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Position; evidence; response; clarification; synthesis; compromise; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Two source excerpts plus group discussion; 12–13 sentences/turns; about 200–214 words.
- **Connectedness:** Sources establish the facts; preferences create the disagreement; mediation produces the final plan.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Zunächst sind wir uns einig, dass …`; `Der strittige Punkt ist …`; `Ich formuliere es noch einmal.`; `Aus beiden Quellen ergibt sich …`; `Unser Kompromiss lautet …`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all Module 15 language and the full B1 discourse inventory.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking checkpoint:** Lead a four- to five-minute supported discussion through evidence, disagreement, clarification, source synthesis, compromise, and confirmed next actions.

## Module 16 — B1 independence and final assessment

### Lesson 91 — One Decision, Several Consequences

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Following a longer personal account about a meaningful decision.
- **Can do:** I can understand and produce a coherent narrative with chronology, motivation, difficulty, and reflection.
- **Primary language structure:** Integrated narrative retrieval; no new structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Background; turning point; chronology; concession; consequence; reflection.
- **Target passage:** Sustained personal narrative; 11–12 sentences; about 194–208 words.
- **Connectedness:** One decision anchors the complete before–event–after–reflection arc.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `rückblickend`; `eine Entscheidung bereuen`; `eine Folge haben`; `anders als erwartet`; `daraus lernen`; `heute würde ich …`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Modules 1–3, personal history, emotions, hypotheses, learning reflection.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening and speaking:** Produce a two-minute structured retelling, then state what the speaker would change.

### Lesson 92 — A Problem at Work or in Training

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Handling a task failure, unclear responsibility, or deadline conflict.
- **Can do:** I can explain a professional problem, distinguish facts from assumptions, and propose a remedy.
- **Primary language structure:** Integrated workplace/process retrieval; no new structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Process; report; responsibility; uncertainty; proposal; agreement.
- **Target passage:** Meeting excerpt and follow-up message; 11–12 sentences/turns; about 196–210 words.
- **Connectedness:** Process evidence and discussion resolve one failure without assigning unsupported blame.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `woran es lag`; `Verantwortung klären`; `einen Fehler beheben`; `künftig vermeiden`; `eine Aufgabe neu verteilen`; `den Stand dokumentieren`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Modules 4–6, passive, source reference, conflict repair, learning strategies.
- **Performance activity:** **Writing:** Produce a 150-word factual update with cause, evidence, remedy, responsibilities, and prevention.

### Lesson 93 — A Public-Service Problem

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Solving a linked transport, housing, health, consumer, or administrative problem.
- **Can do:** I can extract changing practical information and act on conditions, rights, and deadlines.
- **Primary language structure:** Integrated service/problem-solving retrieval; no new structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Announcement; enquiry; evidence; complaint; contingency; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Notice, recorded message, and service dialogue; 12–13 sentences/turns; about 200–214 words.
- **Connectedness:** All inputs describe the same case at different moments and alter the required action.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Nach aktuellem Stand …`; `für meinen Fall gilt …`; `die Frist läuft ab`; `Anspruch auf`; `eine Ausnahme beantragen`; `schriftlich bestätigen lassen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Modules 7–10 and 14, public announcements, formal requests, passive, conditions, complaints.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Solve the case by summarizing status, asking three questions, requesting one remedy, and confirming the deadline.

### Lesson 94 — Presenting a Small Project

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Giving a prepared presentation about a familiar project, proposal, or initiative.
- **Can do:** I can present aims, process, evidence, advantages, limitations, and next steps clearly.
- **Primary language structure:** Integrated presentation and argument cohesion; no new structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Introduction; aim; method; result; comparison; limitation; conclusion.
- **Target passage:** Model presentation; 11–12 sentences; about 202–216 words.
- **Connectedness:** Each section answers one predictable audience question and supports the conclusion.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Ziel des Projekts`; `Vorgehensweise`; `das wichtigste Ergebnis`; `eine Schwierigkeit bestand darin …`; `daraus ergibt sich`; `als nächsten Schritt`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** Modules 11–13 and 15, quantified trends, mechanisms, balanced argument, recommendations.
- **Performance activity:** **Speaking:** Give a three-minute prepared presentation and answer two unscripted but familiar follow-ups.

### Lesson 95 — Rehearsal: A Week of Decisions

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Following connected events across work, home, services, news, and community participation.
- **Can do:** I can retain the main line, select relevant details, infer occasional unknown words, and relay what matters.
- **Primary language structure:** Cumulative B1 comprehension and production; no new structure.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Narration; source; process; opinion; contingency; mediation; decision.
- **Target passage:** Multi-part account with embedded messages and dialogue; 12–13 sentences/turns; about 206–220 words.
- **Connectedness:** Recurring people, dates, and one central goal tie several familiar domains into one week-long arc.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `im Laufe der Woche`; `wie sich herausstellte`; `daraufhin`; `in diesem Zusammenhang`; `letztlich`; `für das weitere Vorgehen`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** the complete B1 sequence, with exact referents and sources made recoverable from context.
- **Performance activity:** **Listening:** After one uninterrupted listen and one replay, create a timeline, source map, and 90-second relevant-detail summary.

### Lesson 96 — Final B1 Checkpoint: Act Independently

- **Module:** 16 — B1 independence and final assessment
- **Real-life situation:** Managing a familiar but non-routine situation involving a narrative, public information, two viewpoints, and a negotiated action plan.
- **Can do:** I can understand clear connected input, explain events and opinions, relay essential information, and collaborate toward a practical resolution.
- **Primary language structure:** Cumulative B1 retrieval only; no new structure and no required use of unreviewed grammar.
- **Sentence functions practised:** Narration; main point; detail; source; opinion; clarification; hypothesis; recommendation; negotiation; confirmation.
- **Target passage:** Narrative, notice/data excerpt, and three-person exchange; 13–14 sentences/turns; about 212–228 words total.
- **Connectedness:** Every input contributes necessary evidence or constraints to one outcome; distractors remain plausible but nonessential.
- **New vocabulary or chunks:** `Die entscheidende Information ist …`; `Soweit ich verstanden habe …`; `Dazu gibt es zwei Sichtweisen.`; `Unter diesen Bedingungen …`; `Wir einigen uns darauf, dass …`.
- **Deliberately recycled:** all sixteen modules plus the A1/A2 foundations required for repair, transactions, time, case, clause order, and everyday vocabulary.
- **Performance activity:** **Integrated checkpoint:** Complete a six- to eight-minute supported task: reconstruct the issue, identify main points and relevant details, distinguish sources and opinion, explain one hypothetical option, mediate two priorities, agree on next actions, and repair one misunderstanding.

### 5. Listening, reading, speaking, writing, and mediation

- **Listening:** Every lesson is audio-first. Input progresses from one-speaker connected accounts to linked announcements, interviews, meeting excerpts, news, and multi-speaker problem-solving. Later tasks require main point, important detail, chronology, source, stance, and relevant-versus-irrelevant information—not word-for-word decoding.
- **Reading:** Texts include formal email, reviews, notices, process guides, account histories, privacy explanations, news, charts, applications, and multi-source packets. Tasks increasingly combine information across sections or sources.
- **Speaking:** Production grows from 90-second narration to two-minute descriptions and opinions, three-minute presentations, facilitated discussions, mediation, and the six- to eight-minute supported final scenario. Repair and clarification remain valid B1 competence, not failure.
- **Writing:** Learners produce connected reflections, plans, meeting reports, complaints, reviews, recommendations, enquiries, applications, incident reports, and source-aware summaries. Typical late-course production is about 130–160 words with models or content notes.
- **Mediation:** Learners brief colleagues and residents, explain notices, preserve source attribution, simplify public information, synthesize two familiar sources, and support compromise without inventing missing facts.

### 7. Passage length, complexity, and stamina

| Stage | Lessons | Planned passage shape | Stamina goal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| B1 bridge | 1–12 | 7–9 sentences/turns; about 104–138 words | Track chronology, reference, and one main development beyond A2 length |
| Early B1 | 13–30 | 8–10 sentences/turns; about 120–158 words | Sustain hypothetical reasoning, processes, and method/purpose links |

These are ceilings and planning bands, not word-count quotas. Passage length must come from coherent information value, not filler. A 150-word passage that requires unexplained specialist vocabulary or dense nesting may be harder than a 210-word clear narrative and must be simplified. Sentence length, clause depth, speaker count, audio speed, numerical density, and reference load must be reviewed together.

### 8. Final B1 “can do” checkpoint

L96 is valid only under B1 conditions: familiar subject matter, clear standard German, recoverable context, limited idiom, and permission to ask for clarification. Success means the learner can:

1. understand the main line and retrieve relevant important details from connected spoken and written inputs;
2. reconstruct a straightforward event sequence, including an earlier cause and later consequence;
3. distinguish fact, attributed information, opinion, and cautious prediction;
4. explain a familiar problem or viewpoint with reasonable precision and connected reasons;
5. compare realistic options, including one hypothetical consequence or contingency;
6. paraphrase an unclear point and check understanding;
7. relay essential information from more than one source without erasing source differences;
8. invite another view, respond constructively, and help negotiate a practical compromise;
9. confirm responsibilities, conditions, and next steps; and
10. repair one misunderstanding rather than continuing from a false assumption.

Perfect grammar, native speed, unsupported abstract debate, implicit cultural interpretation, idiomatic sophistication, and specialist knowledge are not pass criteria.

## Gaps, uncertainties, and decisions for review

1. **Blueprint size:** Ninety-six lessons is an internal mastery-oriented design choice, not a CEFR requirement. Sixteen checkpoints and broad retrieval spacing justify the size; lesson count alone cannot guarantee B1.
2. **Numbering:** This file uses B1 Lesson 1–96. The complete path position is A1 1–48, A2 1–72, then B1 1–96. Production IDs remain uncreated.
3. **Grammar boundary:** Plusquamperfekt, passive variants, dative/prepositional relatives, strong adjective endings, `je … desto`, and selected connectors are placed where they serve B1 tasks. Different programmes may place them differently; CEFR does not prescribe them.
4. **Konjunktiv boundary:** Productive Konjunktiv II is limited to frequent wishes, hypotheses, advice, and politeness. Productive Konjunktiv I is deliberately excluded; reported information uses source frames, `dass`, and indirect questions.
5. **Genitive scope:** Productive genitive is restricted to common possession and transparent high-frequency formal phrases. Rare genitive-governing prepositions and elaborate noun chains are not required.
6. **Passive scope:** Present, past, and modal event passive are productive in familiar processes. Stative passive, passive substitutes, and dense impersonal official prose are mainly receptive unless separately reviewed.
7. **Argument boundary:** Learners give structured positions, reasons, examples, qualifications, and simple counterpoints. Extended abstract debate and fine rhetorical nuance remain beyond B1.
8. **Lexical attestation:** Proposed vocabulary has not yet been normalized and machine-audited against the official Goethe B1 list. Do that before passage authoring, and distinguish productive, receptive, transparent compound, and justified out-of-list items.
9. **Passage metrics:** Word bands are provisional. Audio trials must measure pace, pause placement, sentence depth, speaker differentiation, numbers/names, and fairness of irrelevant detail.
10. **Mediation tasks:** Relay and synthesis must test preservation of essential meaning, source, conditions, and uncertainty—not translation into perfect English or memorization of wording.
11. **Compiler readiness:** Before a candidate uses any new structure, confirm controlled tags, verb data, inflection paradigms, phrase annotations, validation, and rendering support. Extend shared typed logic and tests first; never add sentence-specific compiler branches.
12. **Checkpoint fairness:** Checkpoints assess the preceding language and older retrieval, never the five new support chunks. Rubrics must allow accurate equivalent wording.
13. **Inclusive scenarios:** Work, family, health, finances, housing, administration, and personal history must use varied fictional profiles. No performance should require disclosure of sensitive real information.
14. **Exam relationship:** Official Goethe vocabulary and task shapes are cross-checks, not the curriculum’s organizing principle. Separate exam-strategy practice can be added later without replacing general B1 competence.
15. **External review:** Before candidate generation, a qualified German-language reviewer should validate productive/receptive boundaries, idiomatic chunks, register, and the workload of Modules 11–16.
16. **Publication status:** Nothing here is publication-ready. Every exact passage and translation still requires a sentence-by-sentence audit, complete cards and typed help, audio agreement, Pydantic validation, tests, release validation, and desktop/mobile visual inspection before entering a published-lesson list.
